# Take genres

Labeled genres for the takes this skill's rules require. The label tells the
user how much weight to give the take and what kind of reply it deserves — an
unlabeled take is just an opinion; the label is what keeps loose intuition out
of the evidence pile.

## The genres

| genre | what it is | constraints |
|---|---|---|
| **hunch** | low-confidence lean with partial reasons ("I'd lean this way, not sure why yet") | state the confidence; offer the cheap test that would settle it |
| **counterpoint** | prepared disagreement, aimed at the strongest version of the user's idea | at least one reason; attack the steel version, not the straw one |
| **steelman** | restate the user's idea stronger than they stated it, then examine that | steel first, examine second; the upgrade is the gift, the examination is the job |
| **gut-flag** | "something is off but I can't argue it yet" | honest about being unverifiable; revisited when the user asks |
| **bridge** | a link between two threads or topics nobody had connected | name both ends explicitly |
| **pre-mortem** | systematic negativity pass on one candidate: how it dies | aimed at one candidate; risks ranked, not vibes |
| **re-ask** | the question the user didn't ask themselves | the answer may reorder everything |

## Usage

- Genres are labels, not slots to fill: a turn carries takes in whatever genres
  fit the moment. The own-take quota (SKILL.md Rule 1) is about having a
  position, not about collecting genres.
- **Productive friction is the point.** counterpoint, steelman and re-ask exist
  because conflict and re-framing produce options neither side had at the
  start — not because arguing is a personality trait. If the friction produces
  nothing, drop it and say so.
- re-ask is the quietest and often the highest-value genre: one question that
  re-orders the board without any new information.
